I find the leak by thinking about the man who put the roof on.

Most roofs start leaking inside five years. Not because the work was bad — because it was done right enough not to leak for exactly that long. I have trained roofers. I know how they move across a roof, where the day catches up with them, and which corner gets cut when the light is going. So I work backwards from that.

I respect any and every man on the roof. I am here to protect their efforts. No roof deserves an early death — not after the effort that has been put into it.

Forensic roof repair

A roof is a record of one man's day. The open field goes down early, fresh, and it holds. The cut-in work — valleys, chimneys, pipes, anywhere two things meet and something has to be shaped by hand — gets left for the end, when he is tired and the light is going. That work gets done right enough. Right enough lasts about five years.

I trained roofers. I know how they move, what they leave for last, and what a man tells himself is good enough at four in the afternoon. That is not an accusation — I have been that man on that roof. It is how I know where to look. So I do not start at the stain and work outward. I start with how the roof was built, and I go straight there.

Ninety-five percent of that roof is somebody’s good work. My job is to fix the part that failed and leave the rest of his effort standing.

Then I show you what I found before I quote anything. Sometimes it is an afternoon and the right material. Sometimes the roof is genuinely finished, and you will hear that too — but after I have been up there, not before.

1978

First roof · Still at it

I started in the roofing trade in 1978 and have worked every part of it since — residential, commercial, union, my own company. Some of the roofs I put on in the eighties are still dry. That is not luck. It is what happens when the flashings and the penetrations are done properly the first time.

I stand behind work I did forty years ago. That is a harder thing to say than it sounds, because most of the outfits that did those roofs are not around to be asked.

The short version

  • 1978

    First roofing job, in Illinois, at six dollars an hour.

  • Early 80s

    Conti Roofing in Saddle Brook, then the union side at JP Patti. Learned the trade properly and learned who does it badly.

  • 1982–84

    Strober Brothers, the Brooklyn building supply house. First job in a suit, at twenty-two. The man who hired me is the reason for most of what came after.

  • Late 80s–90s

    Continental Roof Systems, my own commercial roofing company. Schools, federal buildings, flat roofs across New Jersey and New York.

  • After that

    National sales leadership in the finance industry. A roofer in a boardroom, which turned out to be useful.

  • 2009

    Founded the Foundation for Job Creation, a 501(c)(3), self-funded from day one.

  • 2010

    Drove to thirty-nine cities holding rallies about American jobs. Slept in the van. Nobody covered it.

  • Now

    Back on roofs, in Ocean and Monmouth County, doing the part of the work I was always best at.
