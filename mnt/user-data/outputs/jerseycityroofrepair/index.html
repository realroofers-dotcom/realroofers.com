I find the leak by thinking about the man who put the roof on.

Most roofs start leaking inside five years — not because the work was bad, but because it was done right enough not to leak for exactly that long. I have trained roofers. I know how they move across a roof and which corner gets cut when the light is going. So I work backwards from that.

I respect any and every man on the roof. I am here to protect their efforts. No roof deserves an early death — not after the effort that has been put into it.

Hudson County roofs are their own problem

Flat roofs on old brick buildings, brownstones with parapet walls, and a great deal of new construction thrown up fast since the building boom took off. Three different eras of workmanship, often on the same block, and each one fails in its own way.

The leaks are almost never in the open field of the roof. They are at the parapets, the scuppers, the chimney flashings, the pipe penetrations, the places where the new roof was tied into something old. That is the work that gets done last, by a tired man, and it is where I start looking.
